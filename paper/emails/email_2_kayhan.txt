Dear Kayhan Space team,

I'm an independent researcher in orbital dynamics. I recently developed a method to estimate thermospheric density from public TLE data and released it as open-source software (thermoLEO).

Key results:

- The skewness of the LEO altitude distribution in the 823-925 km band collapsed from +0.12 (2014) to -1.85 (2025)
- Lower tail decayed 6.6x faster than upper tail (-113 km vs -17 km over 11 years)
- Multiple regression R2 = 0.986 for skewness as a function of F10.7 and tail quantiles

Why this may matter to Kayhan: improved drag estimation from a no-cost data source could enhance your collision-avoidance accuracy and reentry prediction windows.
