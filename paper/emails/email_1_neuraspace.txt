I'm an independent researcher working on orbital dynamics and space weather. I recently developed and open-sourced a method to estimate thermospheric density in the 800-1000 km range using only public TLE data — no dedicated instrumentation required.

Key results:

- Skewness of the altitude distribution in the 823-925 km band collapsed from +0.12 (2014) to -1.85 (2025), slope = -0.125/yr (p = 0.002)
- Lower tail (q05) decayed 6.6x faster than upper tail (q95): -113 km vs -17 km
- Correlation with the lower tail: r = -0.98 (bootstrap 95% CI [-0.999, -0.787]); multiple regression R2 = 0.986

Why this may matter to Neuraspace: more accurate drag forecasting could reduce false collision alerts in your STM platform. Our method uses only public data, so it can be integrated without proprietary sensor infrastructure.
